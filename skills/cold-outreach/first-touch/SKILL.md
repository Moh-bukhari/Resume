# First touch: earn the reply in three seconds

A first message is judged on three things before it is read: who sent it,
the subject line, and the first line of preview text. If those three read
like every other cold message in the inbox, the rest never gets a fair
hearing. This skill fixes the first message specifically. Follow-ups are a
different job; see the signal-personalization skill for sequencing.

## How to work

1. **Start from the recipient's situation, never from yourself.** Do not
   open with who you are, what you want, or a greeting that says nothing
   ("I hope this finds you well" burns the only line the reader sees in
   preview).
2. **Pick one sharp detail about them.** A real detail from their own
   words, a recent post, a hiring signal, or a visible change in their
   work. One detail, used once, beats three vague ones.
3. **State the consequence of that detail.** What does it usually mean
   for someone in their seat? This is the sentence that proves you
   thought, not just that you searched.
4. **Attach exactly one proof point of yours.** One. The proof must match
   the consequence you just named, not your whole resume.
5. **End with one low-friction ask.** A question they can answer in a
   sentence, or a permission ask. Never ask for a meeting in the first
   message; you have not earned the time yet. Never attach a calendar
   link to a first message.
6. **Cut to the word budget.** Email: 60-90 words. LinkedIn connection
   note: 280 characters maximum. LinkedIn DM to an existing connection:
   under 100 words. If a paragraph is longer than the phone preview pane,
   it gets postponed and then forgotten.

## Subject lines

Short, specific, and tied to the real detail. No clickbait, no
"[First Name], quick question" defaults, no revealing the pitch.

- Trigger as a question: "new deployments lead?"
- Their own words, quoted back: "convert feedback into production impact"
- Their role plus the outcome: "deployments hiring at Retell"
- Flat observation, no pitch: "saw the Grab rollout"

Lowercase is fine. Punctuation is optional. The specific detail is not.

## Opening patterns

Use one. Do not compliment the person, and do not explain who you are
yet; both can wait until the proof sentence.

1. **Event plus consequence.** "Saw you joined as founding Deployment
   Strategist in August. That usually means the team is being built
   around whoever holds the seat now."
2. **Their content, reflected.** "Your post on building the deployment
   team ranked experience last. That is a bar I would rather be measured
   against than a resume."
3. **Hiring signal.** "You have had deployment roles open since summer.
   That usually means the current team is carrying the load while the
   search runs."
4. **Visible change.** "Noticed the team moved its revenue motion onto a
   new system this summer. That usually means the old workflow was
   leaking time somewhere."

## Calibrate the altitude to seniority

Same proof, different message, depending on who reads it.

- **Founder / executive:** the outcome they are accountable for.
  Short. They read on a phone between meetings. Lead with the decision
  or the constraint, not the process.
- **Manager / team lead:** the process they run day to day. What breaks
  when someone leaves, what their team spends time on, what reporting
  takes off their plate. Slightly longer is forgiven if it is specific
  to their team.
- **Individual contributor / practitioner:** the task they personally
  do. Not strategy, not ROI. The concrete work: deployments that land,
  tickets that clear, a workflow that stops being manual.

## Message structure

- Line 1: the specific detail (their words, post, or signal).
- Line 2: the consequence it implies for them.
- Line 3: one proof point of yours that matches.
- Line 4: one low-friction question or permission ask.
- Sign-off: name, email, LinkedIn URL. Nothing else.

## Example (job-seeker voice: terse, direct, no sales language)

> Subject: deployment team build
>
> Your August post on joining as founding Deployment Strategist read
> like a hiring brief: a team being built from zero. I applied for the
> Deployment Strategist role on Friday.
>
> At Tamara I built a pre-ATS recruiting CRM on Greenhouse plus OpenAI
> search over 700K+ applicant records, adopted by TA and the COO. It is
> the closest thing I have to adoption proof for a deployment seat.
>
> Worth a look from someone who built the role?

## Job-seeker adaptation

You are not selling a product, so translate every sales rule:

- The "value proposition" is one proof point, not a feature list.
- The "ask" is small: a read of the application, a name of the search
  owner, or a quick word on the team. Never "15 minutes this week" on
  first contact.
- Work authorization, if relevant, is stated plainly once (for example,
  "the postings list F-1 sponsorship, which is what I need"), never
  buried and never apologized for at length.

## Anti-patterns

- "I hope this finds you well," "I came across your profile," or any
  opener that could open a thousand other emails.
- Company name as the only personalization. Every tool does this, so it
  reads as automation, not research.
- Two or more asks in one message. The reader defers and never returns.
- A feature list of yourself. One proof point, matched to their
  situation, beats a resume pasted into prose.
- Pitching a manager on board-level outcomes, or an executive on
  day-to-day tooling. Wrong altitude, no landing.
- A compliment followed by a pitch. Readers pattern-match it instantly.
