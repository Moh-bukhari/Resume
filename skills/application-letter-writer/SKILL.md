# Application letters

Read `AGENTS.md`, the current bank, the actual JD/resume, and
`docs/LETTERS.md`. Use `skills/application-workflow/SKILL.md` for the combined
packet workflow. For outreach, read Anthropic's original
`skills/anthropic-sales/account-research/SKILL.md` and
`skills/anthropic-sales/draft-outreach/SKILL.md`, then
`skills/marketingskills/cold-email/SKILL.md`; for a formal letter read the
existing `skills/resumeskills/skills/cover-letter-generator/SKILL.md`.
For editing, read Corey Haines' original
`skills/marketingskills/copy-editing/SKILL.md`. Use the clarity, voice, proof,
and specificity passes appropriate to the requested letter. Preserve bank
facts; generic marketing examples and sales-only techniques are not new
candidate evidence. Original upstream skills supply the writing method.

Combine sourced relevance → evidence bridge → one small ask with Corey's
specialist writing/editing methods, using these job-specific defaults:

- Read supplied prior threads and the actual latest exchange before choosing
  cold/warm wording. No history found in checked sources and history not
  checked/unavailable are different states. Never invent familiarity or assert
  this is the first contact from unqueried history.
- Write for the confirmed stage: before application, after application,
  after a sent message, after an interview, or a confirmed offer/other event.
  Unknown application state cannot become “I just applied.”
- Default introductory email: about 100–120 words, flexible up to 150;
  follow-up: 50–90; formal cover letter: 250–350. Change length when the role,
  user, or form warrants; a clear shorter message is fine.
  These override generic upstream length minimums and rigid subject formulas.
- Open with a concrete sourced role/company need, then bridge that need to one
  or two relevant facts from the bank, then one small clear ask. Keep observed
  facts and inferred needs distinct in notes. Warm, direct, and specific;
  avoid exaggerated praise, forced personal hooks, and an achievement dump.
- Save URLs/access dates for employer/recipient facts; save bank IDs for
  candidate claims. No invented mutual connection, past conversation,
  referral, product use, company announcement, or hiring authority.
- Preserve graduation/availability and factual scopes. Do not force a
  location, visa, or experience-gap disclaimer from an upstream example.
- Prefer a real role/context subject over a mandatory “Quick intro.” Use
  contractions naturally. Never assert an attachment or link exists until
  it is included and checked.
- Adapt to recruiter versus team leader: recruiter gets role ID, fit, and
  process context; a function leader gets the problem and relevant work.
- Use `templates/letters/`. Produce the letter type needed now and optional
  variants, not eight unsolicited messages to the same person.
- Keep research notes, uncertainty, source IDs, and word counts outside the
  copyable letter. Unknown recipient details stay explicit placeholders.

Default follow-up suggestion: around 5–7 business days after a confirmed sent
message, adjusted to the employer/user timeline; normally one useful follow-up
with new evidence or a specific unresolved question. Further follow-up needs
a concrete reason. A drafted message starts no timer. Stop a
planned sequence when there is a reply and address that reply instead.

Do not inherit bulk-sales requirements for secondary domains, mailbox warm-up,
tracking, autonomous replies, scheduling, or sending. This task produces drafts.
Do not inherit host-specific silent-work, Page/Slide rendering, upload-date
substitution, or external mailbox-draft instructions. Local drafting does not
authorize external drafts, sends, sales-sequence enrollment, or application
submission. If the user separately requests an external action, follow that
request's scope.

Deliver copyable subject/body, a short explanation of timing/recipient choice,
and any contact verification gap. Save under the application's `letters/`.
