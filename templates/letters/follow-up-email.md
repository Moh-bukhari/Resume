# Follow-up after a sent message

Use only when an earlier message was actually sent and its date/content are
confirmed. Drafting the first message is not evidence of contact. Reuse its
thread when possible. Follow any stated reply timeline and stop if the recipient
declines or asks not to be contacted. A follow-up adds relevant context or a
clear next step; do not send the same pitch through several channels at once.

Keep the final body at 50–90 words. Use one ask and no guilt, artificial urgency,
or reference to tracking opens. If there is no useful new detail, one concise
reminder can be enough. This template does not authorize sending or a sequence.

## Draft record — outside the message

## Subject

Retain the real thread subject. If a new thread is needed:
`Following up on [role title] — Mohammed Bukhari`

## Copyable draft

```text
Hi [First name],

Following up on my [confirmed date] note about [role title]. One relevant detail: at Tamara, I translated recruiting and leadership needs into shared CRM workflows. The posting's focus on [sourced responsibility] is the connection I wanted to highlight.

Would a brief conversation about the role and timing be useful? I expect to graduate in May 2027 and prefer full-time work afterward.

Mohammed
```

Check that the added detail was not already the whole earlier message. If it
was, replace it with another supported, relevant point or shorten the reminder.
If a reply answered the question, respond to that reply instead of using this
generic follow-up. Do not change `message_status` to sent without a confirmed
send action.

If the event is confirmed but its exact date is unknown, omit the date phrase
from the message rather than inventing it. Keep date metadata unknown. Exact
follow-up timing remains pending until the actual sent date is known.
