# After applying: hiring-manager email

Use only after the user confirms the application was submitted to this exact
posting. An application folder or completed resume is not submission evidence.
If submission is unconfirmed, use the pre-application template instead.

Keep the personalized body at 90–150 words. Use a detail from the actual JD and
one compact evidence example. This is an introduction, not a request for special
access or an assertion that the recipient owns the hiring process. Draft only.

## Copyable draft

```text
Hi [First name],

I applied for [role title] on [confirmed date]. The responsibility for [specific requirement from the posting] stood out because I worked on a similar problem at Tamara: building a qualified pipeline while keeping engagement and follow-up connected to recruiting.

During summer 2026, I added 500+ qualified early-career candidates across our sourcing channels. I also ran Sales Navigator event funnels and built CRM workflows linking event attendance, staff interactions, and follow-ups. That experience seems relevant to [Company]'s need for [sourced need].

I expect to graduate from UC Berkeley in May 2027 and prefer to start full-time afterward. Would a short conversation about the role and timing be useful?

Mohammed Bukhari
H7@berkeley.edu
https://mobuk.me
```

Do not attribute all 500+ candidates to Sales Navigator, describe them as hires,
or replace early-career candidates with senior domain experts. Do not say a
resume is attached unless the specific reviewed file will actually be attached.
Update IDs if changing the example. Leave sending to a separately authorized
action; no submission or outreach status changes follow from drafting.

If the event is confirmed but its exact date is unknown, omit the date phrase
from the message rather than inventing it. Keep date metadata unknown. Exact
follow-up timing remains pending until the actual sent date is known.
