# Replies to confirmed hiring events

Choose the single pattern matching a real event and the user's requested
response. These are short drafts, not instructions to send, schedule, negotiate,
accept, decline, withdraw, or disclose references. Do not infer a decision from
an offer, interview, or job-search status. Fill all terms and dates from the user
or the actual thread; leave unresolved decisions as placeholders.

Keep metadata outside the copyable reply. An existing email thread normally
needs no new subject. If replying through another channel, use its current
length limits. Avoid repeating personal contact details unnecessarily.

## Draft record — outside the message

Review company fit using the repository-root `docs/COMPANY-ALIGNMENT.md` guide
before finalizing and before any authorized send. Use the shared packet brief;
keep logistical replies concise and preserve the exact sent version.

Then apply the repository-root `skills/professional-writing-review/SKILL.md`
to the final text: Blader Humanizer, Corey Copy Editing, and a factual/strength
comparison. Record the real review below; a copied template remains pending.

## Interview scheduling

Only offer times the user has confirmed are available. Spell out the timezone
and date; verify daylight-saving conversion if converting a proposed time.

```text
Hi [First name],

Thank you for the invitation to discuss [role title]. I can meet on [confirmed date and time] or [confirmed alternative], both in [explicit timezone]. Would either work for the team?

Mohammed
```

## Offer acknowledgment and clarification

Acknowledgment does not imply acceptance. Name only genuine open questions.
Do not invent the reply deadline, benefits, start date, or an extension agreement.

```text
Hi [First name],

Thank you for the offer for [role title]. Before making a decision, could you clarify [specific question about the written terms]? I am reviewing the offer against the [confirmed decision date and timezone] deadline.

Mohammed
```

If no deadline was supplied, replace the last sentence with a question about the
decision timeline. Keep one central request; combine closely related term
questions in a short list only when needed.

## Negotiation

Draft only around a user-supplied target and a supported reason. Do not invent
market benchmarks, current compensation, competing offers, or leverage. The
seven historical job-search offers in the bank are not current competing offers.

```text
Hi [First name],

Thank you for the [role title] offer. Based on [supported reason relevant to the terms], would there be room to adjust [specific term] from [confirmed offer] to [user-requested target, currency, and period if applicable]?

Mohammed
```

If the user has not supplied a target or terms, record the gap and draft a
question about flexibility without inventing a number. Do not promise to accept
if the adjustment is granted unless that is the user's explicit instruction.

## Acceptance — requires the user's explicit decision and confirmed terms

Use only if the user specifically wants to accept this exact offer. A positive
interview or gratitude for an offer is insufficient. This remains a draft.

```text
Hi [First name],

Thank you for the offer. I accept the [role title] position at [Company] on the terms in [exact written offer/version and date], including the agreed [confirmed start date]. Could you share the next onboarding step?

Mohammed Bukhari
```

If terms are still unresolved, use the clarification pattern instead. Never
insert an inferred pre-graduation start date or work-authorization assertion.

## Offer decline — requires the user's explicit decision

```text
Hi [First name],

Thank you for the offer and the time spent discussing [role title]. I have decided to decline the offer. I appreciated learning about [specific topic actually discussed] and your team's approach to the work.

Mohammed Bukhari
```

Omit the final sentence if discussion details are unavailable. Do not invent a
reason, accepted alternative job, or willingness to reconsider. No ask is needed
for a decision notification.

## Application withdrawal — requires the user's explicit decision

```text
Hi [First name],

Thank you for your time on the [role title] process. I have decided to withdraw my application. I appreciate the time you and the team have spent considering it.

Mohammed Bukhari
```

Use the last sentence only if consideration or contact actually occurred; for an
unreviewed application, simply identify the role and state the user's decision.
Do not send a withdrawal to an unrelated person found through research.

## Reference permission request

Use a confirmed real professional relationship. Ask permission before supplying
a reference's contact details to an employer. A public profile does not establish
that someone has agreed to act as a reference.

```text
Hi [First name],

I am being considered for [role title] at [Company]. Given our work together on [confirmed shared work], would you be comfortable serving as a reference? The role focuses on [sourced responsibility]. I can share the role details and the resume being considered.

Mohammed
```

If permission has already been given, use the actual scope and preferred contact
method; do not reinterpret permission for one employer as blanket permission.
Record the relevant bank IDs if a final reply adds claims about shared work.
