# Interview thank-you

Use only after an actual interview or recruiting conversation. Work from the
user's notes or a confirmed meeting record; a calendar invitation alone does
not establish that the conversation happened. Tailor separately to each
interviewer without inventing what they said or what the candidate promised.

Keep the final body at 80–130 words. Mention one real discussion point, one
relevant contribution, and one low-pressure next-step ask. Do not invent a
deadline or state that the recipient agreed to move forward. Draft only.

## Subject options — choose one

- `Thank you for the [role title] conversation`
- `[Role title] — thank you, [First name]`

## Copyable draft

```text
Hi [First name],

Thank you for the conversation on [confirmed interview date]. Your explanation of [specific point actually discussed] helped me understand what the [role title] team needs to accomplish.

The discussion about [actual challenge] connects to my work at Tamara, where I mapped recruiting and leadership requirements into shared CRM workflows. I would approach [the discussed challenge] by first [one clearly proposed step, not a claim about completed company work]. That is the part of the role I would be interested in exploring further.

Could you share the next step when the team is ready?

Mohammed Bukhari
H7@berkeley.edu
```

If next steps were already specified, replace the closing question with one
appropriate action or omit it so the recipient need not repeat them.
Mention a promised artifact only when that promise is confirmed and the artifact
exists. Availability need not be repeated if already settled; otherwise preserve
expected May 2027 graduation and do not imply an agreed start date.

If the event is confirmed but its exact date is unknown, omit the date phrase
from the message rather than inventing it. Keep date metadata unknown. Exact
follow-up timing remains pending until the actual sent date is known.
