# Align every resume and message with the company

Mohammed wants every tailored resume and job-search message to show how his
experience serves that specific company's mission and goals. Apply this step
when drafting or revising materials and before any separately authorized send
or submission. Use it alongside [tailoring](TAILORING.md), [letters](LETTERS.md),
and [the application workflow](../skills/application-workflow/SKILL.md).

## Build the connection before writing

1. **Understand the company.** Read its official mission/about page, product
   pages, and full job description. Check relevant recent company announcements
   or supplied conversations when they clarify current priorities. Identify
   who it serves, the problem it solves, and the outcome it wants to create.
2. **Identify this role's contribution.** Select two or three concrete goals
   the role or team supports: for example, expert supply, deployment adoption,
   reliable integrations, or customer growth. Distinguish stated goals from
   your interpretation. Funding, popularity, or a mission slogan alone does
   not establish a team's current priority or success metric.
3. **Map goals to evidence.** For each priority, choose supported bank bullet
   IDs, explain their relevance, and retain their limits. Show direct experience
   or a credible transferable skill. Do not turn an adjacent project into
   domain expertise, a demo into a production deployment, or personal search
   results into employer hiring results.
4. **Choose the positioning.** Write one plain sentence explaining how Mohammed
   could contribute to that mission through this role, grounded in the selected
   work. A proposed contribution is not a promised outcome or a past achievement.

Save the brief in the packet's `tailoring-notes.md` under **Company mission and
goals**. Record source URLs, observation/provision dates, relevant source text,
and whether each goal is stated or inferred. The packet's `outreach-plan.md`
references this brief and records the goal/proof selected for each message.
This also applies to a letter-only task: add a short brief to the existing
packet notes before writing. Reuse useful research; refresh time-sensitive
claims when revising or preparing to send.

If live sources are inaccessible, use the supplied JD or dated official text,
record the limit, and continue with supported role-level alignment. Leave an
unverified company-wide mission unknown. Do not fabricate research, personal
enthusiasm, product use, shared values, or a lifelong interest. Ask about personal
motivation only when a required answer cannot be supported by existing context.

## Apply the brief to the artifact

| Artifact | How alignment should appear |
| --- | --- |
| Resume | Choose the summary, strongest bullets, projects, and skills that demonstrate contribution to the company's goals through this role. Make relevance visible in the first few lines. Keep Tamara first, accurate titles/scopes, and the one-page layout. A mission paragraph or company slogan is not required. |
| Introductory email, LinkedIn message, referral request, cover letter | Connect one specific company/role goal to one or two relevant achievements, then make a useful ask. Express interest in the work without inventing a personal conviction or claiming to be a “perfect fit.” |
| Follow-up or thank-you | Use the same company context plus the actual latest exchange. Add relevant proof or address an open need; avoid repeating the original pitch verbatim. |
| Scheduling, offer, withdrawal, or other logistical reply | Check the company, role, and conversation context and answer the actual request. Keep it natural and concise; alignment does not require inserting a mission pitch into an administrative reply. |

For substantive introductions, use the company-swap check: if replacing the
company name leaves the message equally usable for an unrelated employer,
make the goal/evidence connection more specific. For resumes, judge the selected
evidence and emphasis rather than requiring the company name in every bullet.
Similar employers may legitimately share relevant evidence.

## Review before delivery and before sending

- The company mission/goal claims have sources and dates, or the unavailable
  research is explicitly recorded in notes.
- The role's contribution is clear, and the most relevant bank evidence is
  visible. Direct and transferable experience remain distinguishable.
- The writing explains fit through concrete work; it does not depend on generic
  praise, keyword repetition, invented beliefs, or a guarantee of success.
- The company, exact role, recipient, application stage, and latest thread
  match. There is no leftover employer name, goal, or attachment from another
  packet. For logistical replies, the context check is sufficient.
- Record the review outcome and date in the packet notes/outreach plan.
  Unresolved material gaps stay visible; copying a template is not completion.

If sending/submission is authorized, perform this review on the exact text
and artifact being sent. Record the actual event only after confirmation and
preserve the sent/submitted version. If the user reports a message already sent,
record what actually happened; apply this guidance to the next message rather
than rewriting its history. These instructions add a content requirement, not
permission to send or apply.
